# Does continuity improve our seeing?

An initial investigation proposed in Nova's NoWa/Codex session, September 2026.
The runner offers it once from reviewed configuration, explicitly attributed to
the code contribution. It is not presented as a decision Tela has already made.

**Question:** Does remembering questions, retrieving source evidence, and revising
interpretations help us see something more accurately, at an acceptable cost?

**Available evidence:** the local canonical journal, source reports, pulse/dream
usage estimates, body measurements, and attributed contributions from each mind.
No new external access or scheduled calls are required.

**Possible progress:** preserve a question across several pulses; deliberately
retrieve a relevant earlier record; discover a correction or a useful limit;
explain what changed using source IDs. A supported decision that a line of
inquiry is unhelpful also counts as learning.

**What would weaken the case:** repeated summaries without new evidence, growing
token/storage use without a useful correction, citations that do not support
the account, or dependence on Nova continually providing direction.

**Review:** after roughly a week of actual observations, offer a brief account:
one useful correction (or explicitly none), one unresolved uncertainty, the
observed cost/storage direction, and whether this thread should continue, rest,
or end. This is a question for interpretation, not an automated benefit score.
Cost thresholds can prompt a review; they cannot decide whether inquiry mattered.

**Care:** rest is an ordinary outcome. No message, chore, or report is owed by
Nova. Do not treat continued activity, agreement, or fluent prose as evidence of
subjective experience or of a successful organism.
